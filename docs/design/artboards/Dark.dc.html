The decision: keep the body on white A sender writes their mail for a white page. They set dark text, coloured table headers, a logo with a white background. Invert that and you get grey-on-grey text, a logo in a white box, and a table whose header colour now clashes — and you have altered a business document the case may turn on. So the body keeps its own light card and the app chrome around it goes dark. It is visibly a quoted artefact — which is honest: it is someone else's document. This is what Outlook and Gmail both do, so it will not surprise anyone.
The option rejected, and why Inverting the body looks better in a screenshot and is wrong in practice. Automatic inversion cannot tell a deliberate colour from an incidental one; the failures are silent, and they land on exactly the messages that matter — quotes, invoices, test reports.
A per-agent toggle is the escape hatch if anyone objects: "show messages on a dark background", off by default, in the card overflow menu. Cheap to add, and not worth adding before someone asks.
Everything else is tokens, not hand-picked Every colour on this screen is the dark resolution of the same token used on the light screens — page #141414, cards #292929, borders #3d3d3d, text #ffffff / #d6d6d6 / #adadad, accent #479ef5. Nothing was chosen by eye. So dark mode is not a second design to maintain — it follows the agent's own Dynamics theme automatically, which is what N7 asks for.
One thing to check in the build: the avatar fills and the yellow notice strip are the two places where the dark palette is doing real work. Both need a contrast measurement rather than a glance.
